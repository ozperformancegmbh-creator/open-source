## 1. Google algorithm state: core/spam updates 2025–2026, helpful content in core, scaled content abuse, site reputation abuse, AI content, Quality Rater Guidelines

### Takeaway
Google ran 3 core + 1 spam update in 2025 and, per trackers, 3 core + 4 spam updates in 2026 so far (latest: November 2026 core update, 10–24 Nov 2026, finished one day before this note). There is no separate "helpful content" switch since March 2024; the durable pattern across 2025–2026 updates is that templated/thin/mass-produced content and lead-gen informational content lose, and that AI use is not penalised as such but unreviewed scaled output is treated as spam ("scaled content abuse") and can be rated Lowest.

**Helpful content system → core [>12 months]**
- With the March 2024 core update (announced 5 Mar 2024; core rollout finished 19 Apr 2024) the helpful content system stopped being a separate classifier and was folded into core ranking systems; the same announcement introduced three spam policies: expired domain abuse, scaled content abuse, site reputation abuse. [Google official] — [Google Search Central blog, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)

**Scaled content abuse**
- The policy covers many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings and not help users, regardless of whether made by automation, humans or a mix. [Google official, >12 months] — [Google Search Central blog, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- Google's generative-AI guidance: using generative AI to produce many pages without adding value for users may violate the scaled content abuse policy; AI-assisted content must meet Search Essentials and spam policies. [Google official] — [Google: guidance on using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- On 1 Oct 2026 Google reportedly rewrote the accuracy section of that page, telling sites to have humans fact-check machine-written content before publishing. [third-party report of Google doc change] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-tells-sites-to-manually-factcheck-all-ai-content-before-publishing/)
- Enforcement cases: an ~850,000-URL AI "local news" section (National Today) reportedly received a scaled-content manual action and was removed from the index within days of public exposure. [third-party case study] — [Something Inc.](https://somethinginc.com/blog/scaled-ai-content-manual-action-case-study/)
- One source reports that since April 2026 Google may use public spam reports to trigger manual actions. [unconfirmed secondary] — [bulkbase.ai guide](https://bulkbase.ai/seo/understanding-googles-scaled-content-abuse-policy)

**Site reputation abuse [>12 months; not relevant to OZ unless they host third-party content]**

[Google official] — [Google Search Central blog, Nov 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse)

[Google official] — [Google: Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

### Inferences
- A fault-code library is exactly the page type Google's scaled-content policy and QRG 4.6.5/4.6.6 describe when it is produced from a template with swapped code numbers. The same library is defensible when each page carries workshop-only information. The decisive test is "value added compared with other pages on the same topic", not page count or AI use.
- Vehicle repair/gearbox safety content plausibly falls under YMYL-adjacent "safety" (a wrong instruction can cause a breakdown or an accident). I found no source that shows the QRG naming car repair explicitly; treat pages that advise "weiterfahren ja/nein" as YMYL and give them named Meister review.
- OZ is not a lead-gen intermediary but the actual service provider. The Nov 2026 pattern (lead-gen informational sites down, searched-for brands up) favours a real workshop with brand demand, provided the content is clearly attached to the real business (address, Meister, ZF Service Point status).
- Real, named authors only: a real Kfz-Meister with real photo and verifiable credentials (Meisterbrief, HWK, ZF certificate). Stock/AI headshots or invented experts are now an explicit low-quality signal.
- Publish in batches after human (Meister) fact-check; keep a record of who reviewed what. Avoid publishing hundreds of pages in one go right before/while a spam update runs.

## 2. Fault-code libraries: what ranks durably vs. programmatic thin pages

### Takeaway
No documented traffic case study for RepairPal, OBD-Codes.com, YourMechanic or FIXD could be found; the evidence is indirect. The live SERP for "P1735 DSG" shows that the pages that surface are gearbox-specific specialist pages (mechatronic repairers, with gearbox type, symptom, cause, fix) while a generic template library (Autodoc Club) even assigns the wrong meaning to the code — which is precisely the gap a real specialist can win.

### Cited Findings
- SERP sample "Fehlercode P1735 DSG VW" (25 Nov 2026, US-based search tool, not google.de): results dominated by specialist repairers — ECU Testing ("VW Tiguan P1735 & P1736 Fault – DQ381 Mechatronic Repair", separate pages per model e.g. Skoda Kodiaq), ACtronics ("VAG Fault Codes P173500 / P173600: DSG DQ381 TCU Issue Resolved"), ecu.eu (DQ381/DQ500 pressure/displacement sensor), go-parts, Hella Tech World, tx-board.de forum. — [ECU Testing](https://www.ecutesting.com/common-faults/volkswagen/dq381-mechatronic-causing-tiguan-dsg-gearbox-problems/); [ACtronics](https://www.actronics.co.uk/news/diagnostic-help/vag-error-codes-p173500-p173600-dq381); [ecu.eu](https://ecu.eu/pressure-displacement-sensor-dq381500-312.htm)
- Content these pages share: code + sibling code (P1735/P1736), affected gearbox (DQ381, DQ500), concrete symptom (P1735 disables odd gears 1/3/5/7; P1736 the even gears; limp mode), cause located in the mechatronic (clutch 1 position/pressure sensor), repair path. — [ECU Testing](https://www.ecutesting.com/common-faults/volkswagen/volkswagen-p17bf-dsg-fault/); [go-parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1735)
- The generic German library page at Autodoc Club describes P1735 as a General Motors code (2-3 shift valve), i.e. not the VW meaning. — [Autodoc Club](https://club.autodoc.de/obd-codes/p1735)
- A German workshop competitor already uses the exact pattern OZ plans: kfz-dietrich.com article on "VW/Audi DSG Mechatronik defekt – DQ200, DQ250, DQ381 Diagnose" with a visible line "Fachlich geprüft · Kfz-Meisterbetrieb Dietrich · So prüfen wir Inhalte". — [kfz-dietrich.com](https://kfz-dietrich.com/blog/vw-audi-dsg-mechatronik-defekt-dq200-dq250-dq381-diagnose/)
- Programmatic pages and Google: Patrick Stox (Ahrefs) maintains a risk overview of scaled content abuse for programmatic SEO; reported cases include a doorway-style set hit by manual action after ~4 months with −96 % traffic. [opinion/blog; case details not independently verifiable] — [Patrick Stox](https://patrickstox.com/programmatic-seo/risks/scaled-content-abuse/)
- Deindexing can precede the manual-action notice (Glenn Gabe case study; root cause there was a DNS vulnerability, not AI content). — [GSQi](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/deindexed-and-delayed-manual-action-case-study/)
- Since March 2024 helpfulness is assessed in core systems and weak sections can weigh on the whole site (third-party interpretation of Google's site-wide signals). [opinion/blog] — [Hobo Web](https://www.hobo-web.co.uk/the-google-hcu-helpful-content-update-helpfulness/)
- QRG test raters apply: Lowest if content adds "little to no … added value" versus other pages on the topic. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-quality-raters-content-ai-generated-lowest-quality-454161)

### Inferences
(Rules and templates derived from the findings above; they are recommendations, not sourced facts.)

**Rule set "fault-code page that survives spam systems"**
1. Scope the code to manufacturer + gearbox. Manufacturer-specific P1xxx/P17xx codes mean different things per make (see Autodoc's GM reading of P1735). One URL = one code (plus its twin, e.g. P1735/P1736, and the VAG long form P173500) for one manufacturer family. Put affected gearboxes (DQ200/0AM, DQ250/02E, DQ381/0GC, DQ500/0BH; ZF 8HP/6HP; Mercedes 722.9/7G-DCT) in a table, only where OZ has actually seen the code.
2. Minimum unique data per page (aim for at least 4 of 7, and never publish with fewer than 2):
   - anonymised real workshop case (vehicle, year, mileage, complaint, finding, repair, result);
   - measured values/freeze-frame or diagnostic screenshots (ODIS/VCDS readout, pressures, clutch adaptation values);
   - own photos of the failed part;
   - cost range from OZ's own invoices (min–max, what drives the difference);
   - affected gearbox codes and build years;
   - part numbers / repair variants (sensor, mechatronic repair, replacement, software);
   - "Weiterfahren?" verdict with reasoning from the Meister.
3. Publish only codes OZ actually handles. Start with 15–30 pages for codes with real cases, then grow in batches of 5–10 per month. No source gives a safe number; the constraint is unique data per page, not a count.
4. No near-duplicates: do not create "P1735 Golf", "P1735 Tiguan", "P1735 Passat" as separate pages with swapped model names (doorway pattern). Handle models in one table; a separate page is justified only where diagnosis/repair genuinely differs (e.g. different gearbox).
5. Cannibalisation: one primary query per URL. Fault-code page targets "[Marke] [Code]"; component page targets "Mechatronik DQ381 defekt"; service page targets "Getriebespülung"; cost page targets "Getriebespülung Kosten" only if the SERP for that query shows separate cost pages — otherwise make it a section on the service page.
6. Hub-and-spoke: /fehlercodes/ (hub, filter by make/gearbox) → gearbox hubs (/dsg/dq381/) → code pages → component/repair page → service page with booking/contact. Every code page links up to its gearbox hub, sideways to twin codes, and across to exactly one money page. Breadcrumbs mirror this.
7. Thin candidates: codes with no own data go on the hub as a table row (code, short meaning, gearbox) without their own URL, or are noindexed until enriched.

**Page template (German)**
- URL: `/fehlercodes/vw-p1735` (or the nearest Wix allows)
- Title: `P1735 (P173500) VW/Audi DSG: Bedeutung, Ursache & Kosten | OZ Performance`
- H1: `Fehlercode P1735 bei VW/Audi-DSG: Bedeutung, Ursachen, Reparatur`
- Answer block (40–60 words, directly under H1): what the code means, which gearbox, most common cause, drive on or not.
- Fact table: Code / Langform / Steuergerät / Betroffene Getriebe / Typische Symptome / Häufigste Ursache / Weiterfahren? / Kostenrahmen
- H2 Symptome · H2 Ursachen (nach Häufigkeit in unserer Werkstatt) · H2 So diagnostizieren wir (Messwerte, Fotos) · H2 Reparaturwege & Kosten (Tabelle) · H2 Fall aus unserer Werkstatt · H2 Verwandte Fehlercodes · H2 Häufige Fragen (3–5 real customer questions)
- Author/review box: "Verfasst von … / Fachlich geprüft von [Name], Kfz-Meister, ZF Service Point · Stand: TT.MM.JJJJ · So prüfen wir Inhalte"
- CTA: Diagnose-Termin / Fahrzeug einsenden.

### Gaps
- No documented traffic trajectories for RepairPal, OBD-Codes.com, YourMechanic, FIXD or German libraries were found (one targeted search returned only generic material). SISTRIX/Semrush visibility curves would have to be pulled directly from those tools.

## 3. On-page best practice 2026

### Takeaway
Google's own guidance supports visible bylines, honest "who/how/why" disclosure and correctly labelled visible dates that match structured data; answer-first formatting and tables are practitioner consensus rather than Google rules. Google explicitly says no AI-specific rewriting or "chunking" is needed.

### Cited Findings
- Google asks whether authorship is self-evident and whether pages carry a byline where one is expected; content may be written or reviewed by someone who demonstrably knows the topic. [Google official] — [Google: people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Fabricated author profiles (AI headshots, invented names, false credentials) were added as a warning on 1 Oct 2026. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-adds-fake-author-warning-to-helpful-content-guidance/591806/)
- Dates: show a prominent user-visible date labelled e.g. "Published"/"Last updated"; add datePublished/dateModified on a CreativeWork subtype (Article, BlogPosting); visible and structured dates must match; no future dates; dates must describe the page, not the events in it. [Google official] — [Google: Influence your byline dates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- Google's generative-AI optimisation guide (published 15 May 2026): SEO best practices remain the basis; pages must be indexed and snippet-eligible; unique, helpful content matters most; no need to break content into small chunks; no need to rewrite content for AI systems; llms.txt and special markup are unnecessary; do not seek inauthentic mentions. [Google official via trade press] — [Google: AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/); [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-publishes-generative-ai-search-guide/)
- Human fact-check of AI-assisted text before publishing (Google doc change 1 Oct 2026). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-tells-sites-to-manually-factcheck-all-ai-content-before-publishing/)
- German market example of the "fachlich geprüft" pattern with a linked review-process page. — [kfz-dietrich.com](https://kfz-dietrich.com/blog/vw-audi-dsg-mechatronik-defekt-dq200-dq250-dq381-diagnose/)

### Inferences
- Title pattern: `[Code/Leistung] [Marke/Getriebe]: [Nutzen-Trio] | OZ Performance`, primary term first, under ~60 characters where possible. H1 states the same topic in natural German, not a copy of the title. (Practitioner convention; no Google rule or 2026 study found.)
- Answer-first: 40–60-word definition directly under the H1, then a fact table. This is the format featured snippets and AI Overviews typically lift; Google says it is not required, so treat it as reader-first formatting.
- Author box: real Kfz-Meister as author or reviewer, with a profile page (/team/[name]) listing Meisterbrief, years in gearbox repair, ZF Service Point training, photo taken in the workshop. Add a "So prüfen wir Inhalte" page describing the review process.
- Dates: "Zuletzt fachlich geprüft am …" visible and identical to dateModified. Change the date only when content actually changed.
- FAQ sections: keep 3–5 real customer questions for readers and AI extraction; they no longer produce a rich result (see section 4).
- Original photos/video per page (failed part, diagnostic screen) are the cheapest proof of Experience and the hardest thing for template libraries to copy.
- Cost content: publish ranges from own invoices with the drivers of the range (oil quantity/spec, gearbox type, filter, adaptation). A simple cost calculator (gearbox type → price range) is a plausible differentiator; no evidence found that calculators rank better per se.
- Service page "Getriebespülung" structure: answer block → wann sinnvoll / wann nicht → Ablauf bei OZ (own photos, machine, oil specs) → Kosten-Tabelle nach Getriebetyp → Spülung vs. Ölwechsel → Risiken → FAQ → Meister box → Termin.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 study on optimal title/H1 patterns or featured-snippet paragraph length was retrieved; the 40–60-word figure is practitioner convention, not sourced here.
- No evidence found on ranking effect of author boxes, "fachlich geprüft" labels or cost calculators as isolated factors.
- Current featured-snippet prevalence in google.de now that AI Overviews cover >50 % of keywords: not found.

## 4. Structured data: status of each type (November 2026)

### Takeaway
The rich-result surface for a workshop site has shrunk: FAQ rich results ended for all sites on 7 May 2026, HowTo is gone, self-serving review stars for LocalBusiness/Organization are not shown. What remains useful in Germany is LocalBusiness/AutoRepair (entity and local panel), BreadcrumbList, Article with author and dates, VideoObject and Organization; Google says structured data is not required for AI features.

### Cited Findings
- FAQPage: "As of May 7, 2026, FAQ rich results are no longer appearing in Google Search." Search Console FAQ filter, report and Rich Results Test support removed June 2026; API support removed August 2026. Markup need not be removed; FAQPage stays valid schema.org and Google says it may still use it to understand pages. Before that, since August 2023, FAQ rich results were limited to well-known government and health sites. — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-41298.html); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-to-no-longer-support-faq-rich-results-476957)
- June 2025 [>12 months]: Google phased out Book Actions, Course Info, Claim Review, Estimated Salary, Learning Video, Special Announcement and Vehicle Listing rich results; Search Console reporting for them ended Sept 2025. — [Google Search Central blog, 12 Jun 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-console-deprecated-structured-data-40083.html)
- November 2025: further removals including Practice Problems, nutrition facts, nearby offers; Dataset scope clarified. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-to-remove-more-search-features-including-practice-problems-nutrition-facts-nearby-offers-and-more-464255); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-drops-support-structured-data-types-40386.html)
- HowTo: rich results removed from desktop in Sept 2023 after mobile removal in Aug 2023 [>12 months; unverified primary — known Google post, not re-opened] — [Google Search Central blog, Aug 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)
- Review/AggregateRating: since 16 Sep 2019 reviews about entity A on entity A's own site are "self-serving" and not shown as review snippets for LocalBusiness and Organization; applies to embedded third-party widgets too; markup need not be removed. [Google official, >12 months but reported still in the current doc] — [Google Search Central blog 2019](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/making-review-rich-results-more-helpful); [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/blog/google-announces-big-change-to-local-business-review-schema/)
- 24 Jul 2026: Google reportedly added a review-snippet guideline banning fake reviews and undisclosed incentivised reviews, with manual-action risk. [third-party report] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-bans-undisclosed-incentivized-reviews-sites-face-manual-action/)

## 5. AI Overviews in Germany

### Takeaway
AI Overviews now appear for more than half of German keywords (SISTRIX: 57.9 % on 6 Nov 2026), and studies put the CTR loss for position 1 at roughly 35–60 % when an AI Overview shows. Being cited is no longer the same as ranking top 10 (Ahrefs: only ~38 % of citations come from the top 10), and Google's official advice is "normal SEO": indexable, snippet-eligible, unique helpful content.

### Cited Findings
- Prevalence Germany: 57.9 % of 100 million analysed keywords showed an AI Overview on 6 Nov 2026 (SISTRIX); milestones 10 % (Jun 2025), 20 % (Nov 2025), 30 % (Jul 2026), 40 % (Sep 2026), 50 % (Nov 2026). Keyword share counts every keyword equally regardless of volume. [third-party data] — [OnlineMarketing.de citing SISTRIX](https://onlinemarketing.de/seo/ai-overviews-deutschland-anteil-wachstum)
- Mobile: 61 % of analysed mobile keywords, mid-Nov 2026 (SISTRIX Update Radar via OMR). [third-party data] — [wortfilter.de](https://wortfilter.de/google-ai-overviews-61-prozent/)
- CTR Germany (SISTRIX, earlier 2026 analysis at ~20 % prevalence): CTR of the first organic result falls from 27 % to 11 % when an AI Overview appears; >265 million clicks/month lost by German sites; hardest hit family/baby and health, e-commerce least; Google called the study flawed; Johannes Beus (SISTRIX) said the lost clicks are not the conversion-strong ones. Publication date not shown in the extract. — [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/ai-overview-traffic-einbruch-liste)
- Ahrefs (Dec 2025 re-run, GSC-based): AI Overviews reduce position-1 CTR by ~58 % (earlier estimate 34.5 %). [third-party data] — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)
- Seer Interactive 2026 update: earlier −61 % organic CTR with AIO present; decline levelling off in early 2026. — [Seer Interactive](https://seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)
- Randomised field experiment: AI Overviews cut organic clicks on triggered queries by 38 %. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-cut-organic-clicks-38-field-study-finds/573145/)
- Citations vs rankings: Ahrefs 2026 (863K SERPs, 4M AIO URLs): ~38 % of cited pages rank top 10 for the same query, 26 % in positions 11–100, ~37 % outside the top 100; down from 76 % in July 2025, partly due to methodology and Google's move of AI Overviews to Gemini 3 (Jan 2026). BrightEdge reported ~17 % overlap (Feb 2026). — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10); [DesignRush summary](https://news.designrush.com/ai-overview-citations-drop-ahrefs)
- Google official: AI Overviews and AI Mode use "query fan-out" (multiple related searches across subtopics); requirements are crawl access (robots.txt, CDN), internal-link findability, good page experience, indexed and snippet-eligible pages; Search Console has a Generative AI performance report and a generative-AI features setting. — [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features); [Google: AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- Google's May 2026 guide calls AEO/GEO "still SEO". — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/)

### Inferences
- Assume every fault-code and "was ist / Kosten" query in Germany shows an AI Overview; plan for citation plus brand recall, and measure leads (calls, forms) rather than clicks. The clicks that remain are likely the ones with repair intent, which is consistent with Beus's remark.
- Because fan-out pulls from subtopic queries, a page that answers the adjacent questions (twin code, gearbox, symptom, cost, drive-on) has more ways to be cited than a page that only defines the code.
- Do not block snippets (nosnippet/max-snippet) on these pages; snippet eligibility is the entry condition for AI features.
- Head term "Getriebespülung": expect the AI Overview to answer the definition; the commercial variants ("Kosten", "Bochum", "ZF 8HP", "DSG") are where clicks remain.

### Gaps
- No data on AI Overview trigger rate specifically for automotive/fault-code queries in Germany (SISTRIX sector split not retrieved).
- AI Mode availability/usage in Germany and its share of queries: not researched.
- The SISTRIX CTR study's exact period is unclear (Google's own criticism).

## 7. Technical baselines and Wix

### Takeaway
Wix is not a ranking handicap on performance: in CrUX/HTTP Archive data it passes Core Web Vitals far more often than WordPress. Its real limits are structural (URL prefixes for blog posts, JSON-LD size limits, redirect cap, less control over templates), which matter for a several-hundred-page code library but do not justify a migration by themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Core Web Vitals pass rate by CMS (HTTP Archive/CrUX, Nov 2025; origin-level): Duda 84.87 %, Wix 74.86 %, WordPress 46.28 %; Squarespace ~70 % mobile. Wix rose from ~3 % (Jan 2020) to ~75 % (late 2025). [third-party data, 12 months old] — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/core-web-vitals-champ-open-source-versus-proprietary-platforms/563796/); [HostAdvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/website-builders/wix/wix-vs-wordpress-seo/)
- SEJ's analysis calls Core Web Vitals a minor ranking factor. [opinion] — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/core-web-vitals-champ-open-source-versus-proprietary-platforms/563796/)
- INP replaced FID as a Core Web Vital on 12 Mar 2024; target INP ≤ 200 ms at the 75th percentile. [secondary source; >12 months] — [Plerdy](https://www.plerdy.com/blog/google-helpful-content)
- Wix blog URLs: posts sit under a prefix ("post" by default) that can be renamed but not removed; blog and post prefixes cannot share a name. Store product prefix can be removed. [third-party citing Wix] — [pandacodegen](https://www.pandacodegen.com/blog/is-wix-bad-for-seo)
- Wix structured data: <7,000 characters per markup, 8,000 per page. [Wix official] — [Wix Help](https://support.wix.com/en/article/troubleshooting-your-structured-data-markup)
- Wix: 5,000 redirects per site; platform JavaScript cannot be edited; pages are server-rendered (exception: custom Velo code). [third-party] — [pandacodegen](https://www.pandacodegen.com/blog/is-wix-bad-for-seo)
- Wix offers preset markups for blog posts, products, bookings, events and CMS item pages plus custom JSON-LD. — [Wix Help: preset markups](https://support.wix.com/en/article/understanding-preset-markups-for-your-sites-pages)
- Third-party reviews rate Wix adequate for small-business/content sites, WordPress stronger for schema flexibility and manual control. [opinion; mostly affiliate-style sources] — [HostAdvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/website-builders/wix/is-wix-good-for-seo/)
- Google's requirements for AI features repeat the basics: crawlable, internally linked, good page experience. — [Google: AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

### Inferences
- Build the library as Wix CMS collection + dynamic item pages (one collection "Fehlercodes" with fields for code, gearbox, symptoms, causes, costs, case, images, reviewer, review date), not as blog posts: this gives clean `/fehlercodes/{slug}` URLs, a consistent template, per-item SEO settings and structured data from fields. Verify on the live site that dynamic-page markup variables stay under the character limits.
- Thresholds to hold (Google's published "good" values as last known: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1, at p75 on mobile). On Wix the usual offenders are third-party apps, chat widgets, sliders and oversized images.
- Canonical: one self-canonical URL per code; no parameter/filter URLs indexable; hub filters should not create indexable duplicates.
- Migration away from Wix is justified only if a concrete, tested blocker appears: needed URL structure impossible, JSON-LD limits block required markup, CWV failing because of unavoidable platform code, or need for programmatic templating beyond Wix CMS. Otherwise migration adds risk (redirects, re-crawl) without a ranking gain; no study found showing Wix sites rank worse because of the platform.

### Gaps
- No 2026 (post-Nov 2025) CMS CWV dataset retrieved; HTTP Archive should be checked for current numbers.
- Wix CMS limits (collection item counts, sitemap behaviour for dynamic pages) were not found.
- Google's official CWV thresholds, mobile-first indexing status and HTTPS statements were not re-verified in this session (docs unreachable); LCP/CLS values above are prior knowledge.
- ozperformance.de's actual field CWV data was not measured here.

## 8. Realistic timelines and authority/link needs

### Takeaway
Across the web only about 1.7 % of new pages reach the top 10 within a year (Ahrefs, 2025), but of those that do, ~41 % get there within a month, and low-volume keywords rank faster than high-volume ones. That supports a staged expectation: long-tail manufacturer codes in weeks to a few months, "Getriebespülung" as a head term on a 6–18-month horizon with local intent variants first.

### Cited Findings
- Ahrefs (published 15 May 2025; sample of pages first seen Sept 2023) [>12 months]: 1.74 % of new pages reached the top 10 within a year (5.7 % in 2017); of those, 40.82 % did so within one month; 72.9 % of top-10 pages are older than 3 years (59 % in 2017); high-volume keywords take longer than low-volume ones. [third-party data] — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/)
- Recovery/gains from quality improvements tend to show gradually and often only with a later core update. [opinion/blog] — [Search Engine Journal on helpfulness signals](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-helpfulness-signal-changes/516718)
- Core updates in 2026 arrived roughly every 2–5 months (Mar/Apr, May/Jun, Nov). — [Keywords Everywhere 2026 tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/google-algorithm-updates/2026/)
- For "P1735 DSG" the visible competitors are niche specialists (ECU Testing, ACtronics, ecu.eu, a single German workshop blog), not large publishers. — [ECU Testing](https://www.ecutesting.com/common-faults/volkswagen/dq381-mechatronic-causing-tiguan-dsg-gearbox-problems/); [kfz-dietrich.com](https://kfz-dietrich.com/blog/vw-audi-dsg-mechatronik-defekt-dq200-dq250-dq381-diagnose/)

### Inferences
- Long-tail manufacturer codes ("VW P1735", "P173500 DQ381"): German-language competition is thin and partly wrong (Autodoc). A well-built page on an already-indexed local domain can plausibly reach page 1 within 4–12 weeks with internal links only. #1 is realistic for many codes but cannot be promised.
- Head term "Getriebespülung": competitors will be large portals, chains and oil/equipment brands; expect 6–18 months and a need for external signals (links/mentions from ZF Service Point listings, regional press, forums/clubs, suppliers, YouTube). Target "Getriebespülung Bochum", "Getriebespülung ZF 8HP", "Getriebespülung DSG Kosten" first; the local pack for "Getriebespülung" in the Ruhr area is the faster win.
- Schedule content so that 20–30 strong pages are live and internally linked 4–6 weeks before the next core update is likely; judge results after an update completes, not during rollout.
- Links: no study found quantifying link needs for fault-code long tail in Germany; the SERP make-up suggests topical specialisation outweighs domain size for these queries.

- No keyword-difficulty/volume data for "VW P1735", "Getriebespülung" in google.de (no SISTRIX/Ahrefs/Semrush access).
- No Germany-specific or 2026 time-to-rank study; the Ahrefs study is global and from May 2025.
- No evidence base for link counts needed; domain authority of ozperformance.de unknown here.
